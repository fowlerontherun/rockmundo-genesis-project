Record Labels

Record labels connect artists, releases and commercial support through formal contracts. In RockMundo, a label is more than a name beside a release: it can maintain a roster, manage deals, employ specialist staff and take a defined share of the economics it supports.

AdvancedContractsRoyalties

In brief

Choose a label because the deal and the organisation fit your act, not simply because signing looks like the next career milestone. Compare the displayed terms, understand who receives what share, consider the label's roster and focus, and make sure any advance or obligations still make sense if a release performs more slowly than hoped.

What a label does

A label manages the commercial relationship around recorded music. Player-facing label management can include a roster of signed acts, contracts, releases, staff and budgets. Different labels can therefore feel very different even when they operate inside the same market.

Roster

The label has finite attention and capacity. A roster shows which artists it is already supporting.

Contracts

Artist-label agreements define the commercial relationship and the terms accepted by both sides.

Specialist staff

Label operations can include A&R, production and publicity roles rather than relying on one owner to do everything.

Release support

Labels connect to the release and promotion side of the music economy, so signing decisions should be made with future releases in mind.

Choosing the right label

Do not judge a label from one headline number. Look at its genre focus, existing roster, capacity, reputation and the actual offer in front of you. A smaller organisation with space and a good fit may be more useful than a prestigious label whose roster and priorities point elsewhere.

  • Check whether your act fits the label's musical focus and current roster.
  • Look at the label's existing artists and activity rather than assuming a name guarantees support.
  • Compare the contract terms with the option of remaining independent or considering another offer.
  • Make sure the people authorised to bind a band understand and agree with the deal.

Understanding a contract

Contracts can expose terms such as the artist share, label share and an advance. Depending on the deal shown in-game, there may also be duration, release obligations or other rights and conditions to review. These terms matter because they continue after the excitement of signing day.

An advance is not the same as a gift.Treat upfront money as part of the wider deal. Read the contract view for how the offer is structured and what future earnings or obligations are connected to it.

Life after signing

A contract is the start of a relationship rather than the final objective. The act still needs songs, prepared performers, good recordings, sensible release timing and an audience. A label can support that process, but it cannot turn unfinished fundamentals into guaranteed success.

  1. Prepare material. Develop songs before expensive commercial decisions.
  2. Record well. Use suitable performers, studio time and production.
  3. Coordinate the release. Make sure contract and release plans work together.
  4. Support the campaign. Promotion, media and audience activity still matter.
  5. Review the results. Follow royalties, release performance and the ongoing value of the partnership.

Running a label

For players operating an eligible label, roster strategy matters as much as signing volume. Capacity, genre direction, marketing resources and specialist staff all create trade-offs. A label that signs more acts than it can support can create obligations faster than it creates successful releases.

Think portfolio, not collection.A strong roster should make sense as a business. Consider how much attention, production and promotion each signing may need before adding another contract.

What stays behind the curtain?

Artist fit, commercial strength, label resources, release quality and promotion can all matter in sensible ways. The Compendium does not publish hidden signing thresholds, sales weights, reputation coefficients or a formula for the single most profitable contract structure.

Mechanics without spoilersUse the actual contract terms and label information shown to you. The aim is to make business judgement valuable, not to let a spreadsheet solve every signing.
