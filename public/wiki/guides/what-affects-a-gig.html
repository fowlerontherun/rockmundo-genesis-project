Mechanics explainer

What Affects a Gig?

A gig is not decided by one “band skill” number. RockMundo separates how well individual members execute, how tightly the group plays, how each song works live, how strong the production is, how the band commands the stage and how the audience reacts.

IntermediateLive performanceSpoiler-light

The short version

Prepare the right people, the right songs and the right show. A technically strong band can still disappoint with a poor set or weak stage presence; a charismatic show cannot completely hide missing roles or unprepared songs.

The seven useful layers

1. Member execution

Each attending performer contributes through the role they are actually assigned to play.

2. Ensemble tightness

Cohesion, chemistry, focus and rehearsal readiness affect how well the band functions as a unit.

3. Song performance

Individual setlist songs can land differently depending on preparation, performance and audience fit.

4. Setlist flow

Song order, contrast and the shape of the set can influence how the show develops.

5. Technical show quality

Execution, familiarity, equipment, crew, venue environment and production all contribute.

6. Stage performance

Presence, charisma, crowd engagement, frontperson impact and stage setup affect the live experience.

7. Audience response

The crowd reacts to the whole package: quality, expectations, fit, momentum and some bounded uncertainty.

Readiness comes before scoring

Some problems are blockers rather than small penalties. A show needs a usable setlist and the people required to perform it. The pre-show view is designed to tell you whether the gig is structurally ready before it starts.

  • Setlist exists and fits the intended show.
  • Songs have been practised and rehearsed enough for the band's ambition.
  • Required performers are present and assigned to suitable roles.
  • Health and fatigue are not being ignored.
  • Crew, equipment and stage setup are appropriate for the production.

The setlist is a performance decision

A set full of your highest-rated songs is not necessarily the strongest live set. Think about familiarity, tempo changes, crowd fit, opening impact, pacing and whether the band can actually perform every chosen song well.

Equipment and crew support the band

Production quality can raise reliability and presentation, but it is not a replacement for musicianship. The aim is to remove avoidable technical weaknesses and give the performers a platform that suits the venue.

Useful principleUpgrade the weakest meaningful part of the show. A huge lighting rig is less useful when the band is missing a drummer or barely knows the songs.

The crowd has expectations

Audience size, venue capacity, local popularity, fame, genre/venue fit and ticket context can influence the environment around a performance. The same technical performance can therefore feel different in different rooms.

Mechanics without spoilersRockMundo intentionally separates technical quality, stage quality, audience response, fan/reputation outcomes and momentum. The Compendium does not publish their exact weights or random bounds.

How to improve without spreadsheet gaming

  1. Fix blockers. Missing performers and incomplete setlists come first.
  2. Improve the songs you actually play. Practice and rehearsal should match the upcoming set.
  3. Strengthen the group. Chemistry and cohesion make good individuals work together.
  4. Match production to the venue. Crew and equipment should solve real show needs.
  5. Review feedback afterwards. Use outcome breakdowns to decide what to change before the next show.
